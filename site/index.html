COVID-19 tracker, 2020⁠–⁠2021

This page is an archive. It tracked COVID-19 from March 2020 to January 2021 with data from the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) and is no longer updated; the table's counts run to mid-January 2021. In October 2026 the charts of 63 countries in the table, which had gone missing, were redrawn from the last ECDC file kept, which ends on 10 January 2021, and images that no longer loaded were removed.

How to read this page #

  • Cases and deaths are those confirmed and reported to ECDC each week. How many infections were confirmed depended on how much a country tested, so case counts compare poorly between countries, and both cases and deaths undercount the real toll.
  • Aligned charts start each country's line in the week it first passed 1,000 confirmed cases, or 100 deaths, so that countries hit at different times can be compared from the same starting point.
  • Log scales. The line charts, the scatter plots and several maps use a logarithmic scale, on which each labelled step is ten times the one before. A straight line means steady growth, and equal slopes mean equal rates of growth.
  • Case fatality rate, in the table, is deaths divided by confirmed cases. It is not the chance of dying from an infection, because many infections were never confirmed.
  • Per million figures divide by a country's population. The table uses populations from about 2014, which for most countries differ from those of 2020 by a few per cent.
  • The EU is the 27 member states in the aligned charts and the bubble charts. The table's EU row and the EU's line charts, heat maps and scatter plots from January 2021 leave out Czechia, which the 2020 code missed.

G7 #

The United States and the regions below have up to four kinds of chart. Aligned charts: cumulative cases and deaths, one line per country, from the week each country first passed 1,000 cases or 100 deaths. Bubble charts: how each country's cases and deaths in two weeks compared with the two weeks before, week by week, with a play button. Heat maps: weekly cases and deaths per million, one row per country, week by week from the start of 2020 (week 0) to January 2021. The EU also has two scatter plots.

Charts by country #

Two charts for each country: its cumulative confirmed cases from the week it first passed 1,000 cases, and its cumulative deaths from the week it first passed 100 deaths, both on log scales. The country is the red line, with its total in the legend; the other countries of its continent are dark grey and the rest of the world light grey. Fifteen countries' charts were drawn on 27 January 2021; the other eight, whose charts on the server dated from April 2020, were redrawn in October 2026 from the data to 10 January 2021.

Other trackers #

Other COVID-19 trackers and data sources this page pointed to in 2020. Most have since stopped updating. In October 2026 two links that had come to need a sign-in were replaced by their successors, and two that led nowhere useful were removed.

  • Our World in Data, including total COVID-19 tests per 1,000 people for the world and South America, which the page used to embed (testing data to June 2022)
  • WHO COVID-19 dashboard
  • Johns Hopkins Coronavirus Resource Center (stopped collecting data in March 2023)
  • Worldometer (not updated since 13 April 2024)
  • Datawrapper's collection of coronavirus charts
  • News trackers: Bloomberg, South China Morning Post, The Guardian, New York Times (United States), New York Times, Washington Post, Reuters (United States)
  • Excess deaths: The Economist, CDC (archived by the CDC)
  • Mobility: Google COVID-19 Community Mobility Reports
