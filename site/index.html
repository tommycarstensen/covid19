How to read this page #

  • Cases and deaths are those confirmed and reported to ECDC each week. How many infections were confirmed depended on how much a country tested, so case counts compare poorly between countries, and both cases and deaths undercount the real toll.
  • Aligned charts start each country's line in the week it first passed 1,000 confirmed cases, or 100 deaths, so that countries hit at different times can be compared from the same starting point.
  • Log scales. The line charts, the scatter plots and several maps use a logarithmic scale, on which each labelled step is ten times the one before. A straight line means steady growth, and equal slopes mean equal rates of growth.
  • Case fatality rate, in the table, is deaths divided by confirmed cases. It is not the chance of dying from an infection, because many infections were never confirmed.
  • Per million figures divide by a country's population. The table uses populations from about 2014, which for most countries differ from those of 2020 by a few per cent.
  • The EU is the 27 member states in the aligned charts and the bubble charts. The table's EU row and the EU's heat maps and scatter plots from January 2021 leave out Czechia, which the 2020 code missed.

G7 #

The United States and the regions below have up to four kinds of chart. Aligned charts: cumulative cases and deaths from the week each country first passed 1,000 cases or 100 deaths, one panel per country. Bubble charts: how each country's cases and deaths in two weeks compared with the two weeks before, week by week, with a play button. Heat maps: weekly cases and deaths per million, one row per country, week by week from the start of 2020 (week 0) to January 2021. The EU also has two scatter plots.

About this page #

Made by Tommy Carstensen from March 2020 to January 2021. The code is at github.com/tommycarstensen/covid19. To cite the page: Tommy Carstensen, COVID-19 tracker, 2020–2021, https://tommycarstensen.com/covid19/.

Data: weekly COVID-19 cases and deaths by country, and 14-day notification rates by European region, from the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC), whose data may be reproduced provided the source is acknowledged; tests from Our World in Data, under CC BY 4.0. ECDC has since withdrawn the weekly file by country that this page used, and has revised its figures, so the numbers here may differ from those that ECDC, the WHO or national agencies publish now.
