# scripts

Every script that draws, builds or deploys `site/`, with the 2020 plotting scripts the October 2026 ones import. Run them from the repo root, `python3 scripts/<name>.py`; they find `data/`, `site/` and `tmp/` from their own location. Charts written by the 2020 scripts (`plot_series.py`, `plot_choropleth.py`, `plot_bubble.py`) still go to the working directory, as they did in 2020.

Read the Deploying section of `CLAUDE.md` before running `deploy.py`, and do not run the plotting scripts casually: see `data/README.md` for why.

## Environment

`requirements.txt` pins what these scripts import, at the versions the October 2026 figures were drawn with (Python 3.14). `build_animations.py` and `build_press_videos.py` also need ffmpeg. `plot_choropleth.py` needs `geopandas<1.0` and `pandas<1.5`, which cannot share an environment with the rest: `requirements-2020.txt`.

## Rebuilding site/ on a fresh checkout

The images in `site/` are git-ignored. With the data files of `data/README.md` in place, these draw the ones the pages use:

- `fetch_images.py`: `site/img/`, the flags and continent maps (downloads them from Wikimedia).
- `plot_days100_world.py`: `site/aligned_*.png`.
- `plot_heat.py`: `site/heat_*.png` and `site/heat_tests_*.png`.
- `plot_tests.py`: `site/tests_cases_deaths*.png`.
- `plot_scatter.py`: `site/scatter_EU*.png`.
- `redraw_charts.py`: the table's linked charts for the 63 countries whose 2020 charts were never uploaded.
- `build_animations.py`: `site/anim/europe.mp4` and its poster, from the frames in `2020/archive/`.
- `build_press_videos.py`: the Danish press page's three clips (fetches the .mov files from the server over SFTP).

`site/worldmap/worldmap.json` and `site/bubble/bubble_data.js` are committed, so `build_world_map.py` and `build_bubbles.py` are needed only after a change. Every other file the pages use (the other table countries' charts, `plot_bubble.py`'s no-JavaScript PNGs) is on the server, where `deploy.py` requires it to be, with copies in `2020/archive/`.

## The scripts

- `deploy.py`: uploads the page over SFTP; see `CLAUDE.md`.
- `plot_series.py` (2020): the time-series charts (`days100_*`, sigmoid fits) and the HTML table rows (`table<region>.txt`, which `wrapper.sh` moved into what is now `2020/pipeline/tables/`). Needs `countryinfo==0.1.2`, because 1.0 removed `CountryInfo().all()`. Its `download_and_read` fetches `bsg.csv` only when it is missing.
- `plot_choropleth.py` (2020): the world choropleth GIFs (`covid19_*_logTrue.gif`) and the OWID tests GIFs. The page no longer shows any of its six maps: `build_world_map.py` and `worldmap.js` redraw all six, in its colour maps and log ranges. Needs `geopandas<1.0` and `pandas<1.5`: on pandas 1.5 `transform(pd.DataFrame.interpolate)` fails, and from 2.0 `groupby().resample()` drops the `alpha3` column. Do not regenerate the GIFs: the January 2021 GIFs stay on the server as published.
- `plot_bubble.py` (2020): the bubble charts as PNGs of one week, which the page now shows only without JavaScript. Its daily copies in `2020/archive/` cannot be strung into an animation: the measure changed at least three times between May 2020 and January 2021, and the axes and colour scale rescale every day.
- `build_bubbles.py` (October 2026): writes `site/bubble/bubble_data.js`, each country's weekly cases and deaths from `ecdc.csv` and weekly tests from `owid.csv` for `plot_bubble.py`'s eight regions, and downloads nothing. The data file is committed, because `owid.csv` is not. `site/bubble/bubble.js` draws each `<figure class="bubble" data-region>` from it for every week, with the same player as `scrubber.js`: both axes are fixed log scales from -75% to +700%, and a change needs at least 20 cases or deaths in the earlier two weeks, otherwise the country sits in a strip under the plot (too few deaths) or is listed below it (too few cases).
- `redraw_charts.py` (October 2026): draws, into `site/`, the two line charts of each table country whose thumbnail `upload.py` never moved into `2020/archive/`: 63 countries, whose charts had never been drawn or were from April 2020. It calls `plot_series.doLinePlots` on `ecdc.csv` and downloads nothing. It also draws their thumbnails, which the table has not shown since 27797c4 (it links the full-size charts). `python3 scripts/redraw_charts.py Denmark` is a trial into `tmp/redraw/`.
- `fetch_images.py` (October 2026): saves the flags and continent maps in `data/wikimedia_images.txt` at 120 pixels into `site/img/`, with their authors and licences in `data/image_credits.json`. Wikimedia answers HTTP 400 to thumbnails at non-standard widths, which blanked the page's 45 and 440 pixel ones.
- `build_press_videos.py` (October 2026): the Danish press page's three Folketing clips of 29 April 2020 as MP4s with a poster each in `site/`, their streams copied unchanged from the server's .mov files (fetched over SFTP into `site/` when missing), which the server sends as video/quicktime and Firefox does not play. The page offers the MP4 first and the .mov second.
- `backup_external_images.py` (October 2026): a copy of every image the pages take from other sites, into `external_images/`: the international page's hotlinked images, the tweets' photos (from the Wayback Machine when a tweet has been deleted), PNG and SVG copies of the OWID charts and the YouTube thumbnails, with `manifest.json` and the tweets' JSON committed. One request a second, never to tommycarstensen.com; a second run fetches only what is new.
- `build_animations.py` (October 2026): re-encodes the page's map GIFs as MP4s plus a poster JPEG each in `site/anim/`, cropped to the frames' content (9.7 MB of GIF becomes 1.6 MB), and writes their `<video>` tags to `tmp/build_animations_markup.html`. Europe comes from the 43 PNG frames in `2020/archive/`, the two OWID tests maps from their GIFs; a GIF that `plot_choropleth.py` has written to the repo root takes precedence over `2020/archive/`. The page now uses only Europe's: the six interactive world maps replace the four ECDC world maps and, since 6 October 2026, the two tests videos too. `site/anim/scrubber.js` turns each `<video data-fps>` into a player with play/pause and a slider that steps one frame at a time. Testing it locally needs a server that honours HTTP Range requests, as the live Apache does: Chrome resets every seek to 0 on Python's `http.server`.
- `build_world_map.py` (October 2026): writes `site/worldmap/worldmap.json` from `ecdc.csv`, the tests in `owid.csv` and Natural Earth's 1:110m countries (`data/natural_earth/ne_110m_admin_0_countries.zip`, read with pyshp, in the Equal Earth projection), and the page markup to `tmp/build_world_map_markup.html`; it downloads nothing. The hand-written `site/worldmap/worldmap.js` and `worldmap.css` draw it as six maps in the 2020 page's two rows, `#cumulative` and `#weekly`, of cases and deaths per million people of 2019 and tests per thousand, on one sticky week slider, with tooltips and a table of all 213 countries and territories, 42 of them too small for the maps. Each map keeps the colour map and log10 range of its GIF in `plot_choropleth.py`, on a continuous scale: cumulative cases OrRd, deaths PuRd, tests Greens; weekly cases YlGnBu, deaths PuBuGn, tests Blues. Do not swap them for a generic palette: Tommy rejected the eight-class blue and orange ramps it first had. They replaced the six GIFs on 6 October 2026 (`#world-maps` and `#tests` remain as anchors). Those jumped between frames, because geopandas resets the axes' aspect from the countries without data, and the weekly two showed seven-week totals: their 7-row rolling sum was a week while ECDC published daily rows, and seven weeks once it published weekly rows in December 2020 (the USA in the week to 24 May 2020 is log10 3.61 per million in the GIF, 2.68 for that week). Both are fixed in `plot_choropleth.py`, but do not regenerate the GIFs: the January 2021 GIFs stay on the server as published, and the page no longer uses them.
- `plot_days100_world.py` (October 2026): every aligned figure on the page as small multiples, one panel per country, from `ecdc.csv`: World1, World2, and each region and subregion the 2020 page charted (the EU, Europe, the Americas and its two parts, Asia except China and its three parts, Africa, Oceania, the Nordic countries), into `site/aligned_{cases,deaths}_<set>{,_narrow}.png`, with `<picture>` tags in `tmp/plot_days100_world_markup.html`; `python3 scripts/plot_days100_world.py Africa` draws one set. It replaces `plot_series.py`'s 480x360 `days100_*_perCapitaFalse_<set>.png`, whose 4 to 55 lines shared 10 colours. The country's line is red over grey, as in `plot_series.py`'s comparison charts. Sets of more than 20 get eight columns (three on phones), and a regional figure names its countries that never passed the threshold under it. The World EU is summed under ECDC's names. A new set needs its countries in `SETS` and its title in `REGIONS`. A country with a section of its own (`COUNTRIES`: the United States) gets one figure of its cases and deaths instead, `site/aligned_United_States_of_America{,_narrow}.png`, in red over every other country in grey, which replaces its 2020 pair; `python3 scripts/plot_days100_world.py United_States_of_America` draws it. The run stops if a name runs into its total or text runs off a figure.
- `regions.py` (October 2026): the regions as lists of ECDC names, `plot_series.py`'s of 2020 plus the 24 places they missed; `python3 scripts/regions.py` checks every name against `ecdc.csv` and that every place ECDC reports is in a region. `plot_series.py`, `plot_days100_world.py`, `plot_heat.py` and `plot_scatter.py` read it.
- `plot_heat.py` (October 2026): the regional heat maps of weekly cases and deaths per million people, one figure per region with the two side by side, in `plot_series.py`'s OrRd on one fixed log scale for every region, into `site/heat_<region>{,_narrow}.png`, with `<picture>` tags in `tmp/plot_heat_markup.html`. It replaces `plot_series.doHeatMaps`' `plot_heat_{cases,deaths}_<region>.png`, which rescaled every chart and left Czechia out of the EU. It also draws a tests heat map per region, weekly tests per thousand people (Blues, as the weekly tests map, 0.01 to 100) beside the share of tests positive (OrRd, 0.1% to 100%), with rows only for places with a test count and no share where cases exceed tests (20 weeks in Peru, Brazil and Ecuador), into `site/heat_tests_<region>{,_narrow}.png` and `tmp/plot_heat_tests_markup.html`; the page shows each under its region's cases and deaths heat map.
- `plot_tests.py` (October 2026): weekly tests, cases and deaths per million people for 16 countries, one panel each, on one log scale, so the gap between tests and cases is the share of tests positive; into `site/tests_cases_deaths{,_narrow}.png`, with its `<picture>` tag in `tmp/plot_tests_markup.html`. Its colours are Dark2 from `plot_series.define_colors`, the hues of the 2020 bar charts' Set2. The page shows it in "Tests, cases and deaths" (`#testing`), between the world maps and the table. Both it and the tests heat maps take their tests from `build_world_map.read_owid_tests`, which since 31a5d2f falls back to OWID's daily counts for the 8 places without a usable running total (France, Sweden and Czechia among them).
- `plot_scatter.py` (October 2026): the EU's scatter plots, each member state's highest weekly cases and deaths against its population on log scales, with dashed lines at equal weekly rates per million, from `ecdc.csv`, into `site/scatter_EU{,_narrow}.png` (side by side, or stacked on phones), with its `<picture>` tag in `tmp/plot_scatter_markup.html`. It replaces `plot_series.doScatterPlots`' `scatter_EU_{cases,deaths}.png`, which left out Czechia. It names every point, with a leader line where points crowd, tries 31 orders of placing the names and keeps the one with the fewest and shortest leaders (about 50 seconds), and stops if a name finds no room; a failed run saves a draft to `tmp/scatter_EU{,_narrow}_draft.png`.

## Checks

`setup.cfg` holds the pycodestyle settings (docstrings and comments are one line per paragraph, so E501 is ignored) and `ruff.toml` tells ruff's import sorting that `scripts/` holds the project's own modules. Every script here is clean under ruff, pycodestyle and pyright.
